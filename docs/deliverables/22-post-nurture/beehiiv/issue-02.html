The Reach Channel Most Founders Skip

Hi {{first_name}},

The Leak

Most webinars don't have a content problem. They have a reach problem.

You post. You email your list. Maybe you run a few ads. And the same few hundred people see it every single time.

If the room is small, a better slide deck won't fix it. More of the right people hearing about it will.

The Fix: Calendar-Invite Outreach

A calendar invite shows up as an event: a date, a time and a Yes, No or Maybe button. It asks for one small decision, not a reply.

Here's how we run it:

  1. Build the list. The people your offer is for, by role and type of company. Nobody else.
  2. Write the event description like a landing page. The promise, who it's for, what they leave with. It's often the only thing they read.
  3. Send from dedicated accounts and domains, never your main one, and keep each account's daily volume steady.
  4. Move every Yes and Maybe to your registration page, and from there into your reminder ladder (last week's issue).
  5. Track reach, RSVPs, registrations and live attendees in one sheet, every week.

The Proof

Growth Idea's biggest webinar with us reached 99,859 people. 98,000 of them came from calendar invites.

FintechCircle, a London fintech network, sent 38,138 calendar invites for the fourth episode of its series and got 187 Yes and 221 Maybe RSVPs.

Do This This Week

  • Rewrite your next event description as if it's the only thing people will read.
  • Count how many people your last webinar actually reached.
  • Pick one sending account that isn't on your main domain.
