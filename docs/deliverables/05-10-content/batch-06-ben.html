Carousels

BE1. You Don't Have to Trust Your AI Agent. You Have to Read Its Log.

  • Format: Carousel, 9 pages. Sample HD07 (Harry Dry #7): Victorian collage with serif "You don't have to..." lines, aged paper, floral sprigs and gold corners; the antique figures become antique objects (ledgers, quills, a balance scale), so no people appear.
  • Hook: "The agent says it worked" is not evidence. Here is what evidence looks like when an AI builds your funnel.
  • Lesson: Every command runs through one recorder that appends the command and its full, unedited output to a checks file. A lint fails any edited log. The final report quotes only numbers found in that file. You don't have to watch every run; you have to read one file.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The live session shows AI building the system; this is how you check its work afterwards.
  • Source: context/from-tantra-content/06-revops-ai-systems.md §1.3 steps 5 and 7.

BE2. 7 Cards That Predict a Broken Campaign

  • Format: Carousel, 10 pages. Sample HD08 (Harry Dry #8): left text column with a purpose-built tarot card on the right and pill callouts; the psychology cards become failure cards, each paired with the check that catches it.
  • Hook: Most broken campaigns were broken before they sent. Seven failures you can catch while it is still a draft, in any tool.
  • Lesson: Run this by hand on any campaign before it starts: a merge field left raw ({{firstName}} showing); a sentence that breaks when the company name is missing; two links in one message; no opt-out line; a step with no exit (a reply or a booking should stop it); a link nobody has clicked in a real browser; a booking link that shows no open slots. Each card: the failure, then the check.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session's nurture and outreach stages are built as drafts first; this is what a draft must pass.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §3.1 (copy rules: merge fields that read well when missing, one link, the opt-out line; the pre-launch gate: a booking link opened in a real browser that shows open slots), §13.3 (one exit rule per sequence).

BE3. Same Rule at 10 People or 10,000

  • Format: Carousel, 8 pages. Sample HD09 (Harry Dry #9): one fixed hand-drawn frame, one escalating variable; the crowd becomes a queue of messages that grows from a room to a stadium while the scheduler in the corner never changes. No faces (the crowd is drawn as envelopes).
  • Hook: Your reminders worked for 40 registrants. At 4,000, one rerun of a scheduler that keeps no send record means 4,000 duplicates.
  • Lesson: Design for the rerun. The scheduler runs every 5 minutes and records every send, so each message goes to each person once however often it runs; a repeated email registers once, not twice; sends are capped per mailbox per day, so a big list is paced over days instead of fired at once. At 10 people a duplicate is an apology; at 10,000 it is thousands of them. (The reminder timing rules themselves are Aquila's AQ4; this is the plumbing under them.)
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session's show-up sequence depends on these rules; scale is where they get tested.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §2.2 (the scheduler sends each message to each person once), §3.7 (a repeated email registers once), §3.10 (per-mailbox daily caps).

BE4. Two Test Contacts: One Who Came, One Who Missed

  • Format: Carousel, 9 pages. Sample HD25 (Harry Dry #25): two friendly 3D mascots on the cover, then an annotated 2x2 grid with hand-drawn arrows; the mascots are the two test contacts (non-human), the grid shows what each must receive.
  • Hook: You will not find your funnel's worst bug with a real attendee. You find it with a fake one who never shows up.
  • Lesson: Run the whole funnel with two test inboxes: one attends, one never does. Move the clock forward for test contacts only, at most 10 per call. Every message that goes down the wrong branch is a failure. The quiet failure is the missing no-show tag: nothing errors, the no-show emails simply never go.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session launches one small run; this test comes right before it.
  • Source: 06-revops-ai-systems.md §5 layer 7 (tests before real people).

BE5. Make Every Setup Step Safe to Run Twice

  • Format: Carousel, 8 pages. Sample AB17 (Austin Belcak #17): fake-tweet comparison cards, cross column against tick column; the two job-seeker types become two kinds of deploy script. Tweet header: avatar needed (photos from Adi), or name line only.
  • Hook: If running your setup twice creates two pipelines, two tag sets or two databases, you will never dare run it again. And you will need to.
  • Lesson: Whatever builds your setup (a script, a Zapier or Make scenario, an AI agent), make it look before it creates: find the pipeline, tag, field or campaign by its exact name and reuse it; write down what it created. Test it the simple way: run it twice and check nothing doubled. Then a rerun after a fix is routine, not a risk.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session shows the system going live; this is how you change it after launch without breaking it.
  • Source: 06-revops-ai-systems.md §1.4 (setup scripts that are safe to rerun and match by exact name).

BE6. 7 Things Your Registration Form Should Record That Nobody Sees

  • Format: Carousel, 9 pages. Sample AB42 (Austin Belcak #42): numbered one-column text slides, imagery on the cover only; the sample's writing figure becomes an illustrated form with hidden fields peeled back, no person.
  • Hook: Your form asks for a name and an email. It should also be quietly saving seven things the visitor never types.
  • Lesson: Hidden fields on every registration: utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign, utm_content, the visitor's time zone (so the day-before reminder lands at 08:00 their time), the headline variant they saw, and the Google and Meta click ids. Without them, attribution and timing are guesses.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session builds the registration page; these fields are what make every later stage measurable.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §14 (hidden form fields, landing_variant) and §2.2 (time zone rule).

Animated Image Posts

BE7. Done When: Every Line Ends in PASS or FAIL

  • Format: Animated image, 1080 x 1350 loop (MP4, GIF fallback). Sample AV06 (Alex Vacca #6): flat isometric editorial illustration of a key being pressed, with grain; the key becomes Enter. Motion: the key presses and a column of "done when" lines stamps PASS, PASS, FAIL.
  • Hook: "Mostly done" is how a funnel ships with a broken branch. Write what done means before you build.
  • Lesson: Every task lists its "done when" lines before work starts. The finish is one block of evidence per line, each ending PASS or FAIL. One FAIL means not done, and the status says what waits and on whom.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session walks through each stage's build; this is how each one is signed off.
  • Source: 06-revops-ai-systems.md §1.2 (task guides with Done when) and §1.3 steps 5 and 6.

BE8. The Road From Test Mode to Real Money

  • Format: Animated image, 1080 x 1350 loop (MP4, GIF fallback). Sample AV10 (Alex Vacca #10): serpentine board-game path through numbered level cards from START to GOAL; each level is one gate before live payments. Motion: a walker moves level to level; the "LIVE" flag at the goal lights only when the last gate is passed.
  • Hook: Switching payments to live is one click. Put that click at the end of the board, not the start.
  • Lesson: The gates, in order: build the checkout in test mode; run the full test run until every branch passes; wait for the payment provider to approve the account; the owner says yes to taking real money, in their own words; swap to live keys; launch one small run before the full list. Skip a gate and the first real buyer finds the bug.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session's last build step is going live; this is the order that makes the first real payment a non-event.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §13.3 (build in test mode, swap to live only after the test run, provider approval and the owner's yes); 06-revops-ai-systems.md §1.3 (Day 3: launch one small run).

  • Format: Animated image, 1200 x 627 landscape loop as the sample (MP4, GIF fallback).
