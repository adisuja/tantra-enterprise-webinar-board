Carousels

AQ1. Every Automation Is Proven, Failing or Not Built. There Is No Fourth Status.

  • Format: Carousel, 8 pages. Sample AV31 (Alex Vacca #31): storytelling through a pixel-accurate chat-tool UI, the agent's status digest shown in context rather than described; logo and initial tiles instead of faces.
  • Hook: "It should work" is not a status. Your automation list needs three words: proven, failing with a reason, or not built.
  • Lesson: Keep one register of every automation in the funnel (confirmation, reminders, the attended and no-show branches, sales follow-ups, sync). Each line carries one of three statuses and the evidence behind it: proven means a test contact went through it and the log shows it; failing means it ran and the reason is written down; not built means not built. Recheck the register after any change to a page or a message, and before every launch.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session shows the register for its own funnel; the CTA invites readers who are not sure which of their automations actually fire.
  • Source: context/from-tantra-content/06-revops-ai-systems.md §3.4; 05-funnel-systems.md §10 (weekly review step 5).

AQ2. One Generic Join Link and You Can't Tell Who Attended

  • Format: Carousel, 8 pages (cover, the steps, a close page; the engine minimum is 8). Sample HD31 (Harry Dry #31): left text, right object; a centred "introducing" page with an annotated mock record and hand-drawn callout arrows.
  • Hook: If everyone joins through the same link, your attended list is a guess. And every follow-up after the session is built on it.
  • Lesson: Give every registrant a personal join link. The personal link is what turns a join into an attended tag, and that tag decides who gets the offer email and who gets the replay. With one generic link, attendees get replay emails and no-shows get the offer. The annotated record shows the chain: registration, personal link, join, tag, the right branch.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: Every registrant for this session gets their own link; the CTA points out that registering shows the mechanism from the inside.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §2.2 (the event room).

AQ3. WhatsApp Reminders: What to Write and What Gets You Blocked

  • Format: Carousel, 8 pages. Sample HD23 (Harry Dry #23): colour-coded DON'T over DO cards, each DO box holding a line you can copy and paste.
  • Hook: A WhatsApp reminder with urgency words stops being a reminder. It becomes marketing, and people block marketing.
  • Lesson: Four DON'T/DO pairs. DON'T: "Last chance, don't miss out!" DO: "The session starts tomorrow at 11:00. Here is your link." DON'T: two links (a choice at 15 minutes is a lost attendee). DO: one link. DON'T: message anyone who didn't tick the WhatsApp box. DO: only opt-ins. DON'T: leave out the exit. DO: end with "Reply STOP to stop these messages." and turn consent off the moment STOP arrives.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: Registrants who tick the WhatsApp box get exactly these messages; the session covers the full show-up sequence.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §3.4 and §2.2 (one link per message).

AQ4. Four Clock Rules That Stop a Reminder From Arriving Late

  • Format: Carousel, 8 pages. Sample AB16 (Austin Belcak #16): the fake-tweet card container with bold inline emphasis, one numbered rule per card. Avatar needed (photos from Adi), or name line only (no photo).
  • Hook: A "starting in an hour" message that arrives after the session ended is worse than no message. Four rules that make it impossible.
  • Lesson: (1) A message due before the start is never sent after the start. (2) "We're live" gets a 15-minute grace; any other message gets a 6-hour window, then it is dropped, never sent late. (3) Late registrants skip the messages they missed by design; the confirmation, 1-hour, 15-minute and live messages carry them. (4) The day-before reminder goes at 08:00 in the registrant's own time zone. (Sending each message once is BE3; leaving every branch on payment is AQ10.)
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: These rules run behind this session's reminders; the CTA invites readers to register and watch the timing land.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §2.2 (timing rules the funnel enforces).
  • New vs registry: T3 listed the 13 messages and when each goes. This idea is the rule set that governs failure (late, duplicate, wrong person), not the message list.

AQ5. The 30-Second Call That Starts Your Session

  • Format: Carousel, 8 pages (cover, six rules, a close page). Sample AB46 (Austin Belcak #46): a tweet-style cover card, then numbered chapters, one rule per chapter. Avatar needed (photos from Adi), or name line only (no photo).
  • Hook: An automated call at go-live can pull people into the room, or it can feel like spam. Six rules decide which one you built.
  • Lesson: Call only people who ticked "Call me when the session starts" (the box is never ticked by default). Say first that it is an automated call from your brand. Keep it to 40 to 70 words, under 30 seconds aloud. Say only that the session is starting now and the link is already in their WhatsApp and email. No offer, no question, no links or numbers to type. Call each person once.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session's show-up sequence ends at the start time; this is its last touch, for people who asked for it.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §3.6 (AI voice call at go-live).

AQ6. Follow Up by How Much They Watched, Not by the Clock

  • Format: Carousel, 8 pages. Sample HD41 (Harry Dry #41): chip grids per band, one chart page for credibility (an unlabelled watch-depth curve marked as an illustration, not data), and one full-bleed illustrated page.
  • Hook: A viewer who watched 10% and one who watched 90% should never get the same email. Sort them by depth instead.
  • Lesson: For a recorded presentation, the follow-ups are triggered by watch depth 24 hours after opt-in, never by a timed set running alongside. Under 25%: one re-engage with the two minutes worth watching and the minute to skip to, then let them go. 25 to 75%: the missing piece, with its timestamp. 75% or more and Part 2 not reopened: straight to Part 2. A low-depth re-engage is a different hook, not a louder version of the first. Judge each band by its opt-in rate.
  • Proof: None (method only; the curve carries no numbers).
  • Webinar tie: The session compares the live and recorded shapes; the CTA invites readers deciding between them.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §2.3 (depth-triggered follow-ups).

AQ7. The Three Hours After Your Session Ends Decide Who Gets the Offer

  • Format: Animated image, 1080 x 1350 loop (MP4, GIF fallback). Sample AV27 (Alex Vacca #27): a horizontal node timeline on a connector rail, grouped glass cards below, a cursor that moves across the panels. Motion: the cursor walks the rail from "session ends" to "+24 hours", lighting each node.
  • Hook: Your session ended an hour ago. Does your funnel know who came? If not, someone is about to get the wrong email.
  • Lesson: End of session: attendance reports start arriving. 3 hours after the end: everyone registered without an attendance record is tagged no-show; a later attendance report clears a wrong one. Within 6 hours: attendance must be in, or the attendee offer email is dropped. +24 hours: the first no-show email (the replay) goes. If your platform has no attendance webhook, run the participant pull inside 3 hours, every time.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: This is the clock that runs after this session; the CTA invites readers to register and see which branch they land in.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §2.2 (the event room, timing rules).

AQ8. How an AI Builds Your Funnel One Chat at a Time Without Forgetting Anything

  • Format: Animated image, 1080 x 1350 loop (MP4, GIF fallback). Sample AV01 (Alex Vacca #1): a hero terminal and chat mock with a burst node fanning into three labelled columns, orange highlight boxes on key words. Motion: the terminal types one prompt, then each column lights in turn.
  • Hook: Your AI forgets everything when the chat closes. Your files don't. Here's the loop that builds a funnel one chat at a time.
  • Lesson: Column 1, start: restate the task in two lines; search the saved answers before asking; ask once, in one message, for everything missing. Column 2, build and prove: build to the task guide; run every command through a script that appends it and its unedited output to a log. Column 3, hand over: tick the status file (done, or waiting on the owner with the exact click path) and end on a short report that quotes only numbers found in the log, plus the one next prompt.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session shows the build running this way; the CTA is for founders who have tried building with AI and lost the thread between chats.
  • Source: 06-revops-ai-systems.md §1.3 (how a session runs).
  • New vs registry: T6 taught the guardrails that stop an agent acting without approval. This idea is the session loop (restate, ask once, prove with a log, hand over), with no guardrail repeated.

AQ9. Your CRM Should Never Send a Message

  • Format: Animated image, 1080 x 1350 loop (MP4, GIF fallback). Sample AV47 (Alex Vacca #47): a vertical flow from one hero node into three lanes, a folder-tree metaphor and UI cards below. Motion: one contact dot travels down from registration through the lanes into the CRM card and a stage move travels back up.
  • Hook: If your CRM and your funnel both send follow-ups, every lead gets two of everything. Give each one a single job.
  • Lesson: One sender per message. The funnel (or your sending tool) sends every email, WhatsApp message and reminder; the CRM's automations only create tasks and internal alerts for people. Audit it today: list every CRM workflow that messages a lead, turn each one into a task or an alert, and check the funnel already sends that message.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session's pipeline stage shows the sync live; the CTA is for teams whose leads already get doubles.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §13.2 ("CRM automations only create tasks and internal alerts; the funnel and Tantra send every message").

AQ10. The Exit Every Buyer Needs: Leave Every Branch the Moment They Pay

  • Format: Animated image, 1080 x 1350 loop (MP4, GIF fallback). Sample AV48 (Alex Vacca #48): a tall, navigable system map of every branch, wired with connectors and no visual chaos, cropped to a 4:5 hero with the full map in the first comment. Motion: a buyer dot enters at a purchase and every branch it was in greys out in sequence.
  • Hook: Nothing loses a new customer faster than a "last chance to book" email the day after they paid. Map every branch they must leave.
  • Lesson: Draw every branch a contact can sit in at once: before the session, after it (came or missed), the sales follow-ups, the long-term nurture. One event must cut across all of them: the moment someone pays, they leave every chasing branch at once, and a refund puts them back. Check it by buying with a test contact halfway through a sequence and watching every branch let go.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session ends with the full system map; the CTA invites readers to see their own branches on it.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §2.2 ("a buyer leaves every branch at once"), §13.2 (purchased removes the contact from every chasing branch).
