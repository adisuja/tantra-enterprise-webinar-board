Carousels

BK1. The Anatomy of a Cold Calendar Invite That Registers People

  • Format: Carousel, 9 pages. Sample HD18 (Harry Dry #18): the annotated teardown grid, a bright cover with an angled device mockup, then one labelled part of the invite per page.
  • Hook: A cold calendar invite lands inside someone's own calendar. Most get declined because they read like an ad. Here's every line, labelled.
  • Lesson: Name the title like a meeting (plain words and the topic; no $, no %, no "free"). Line 1 is the date, time and time zone in words, because each guest's own calendar does the reminding. Lines 2 and 3: who it is for and what they leave with. Then one line that makes declining easy ("If it's not relevant, just decline. No follow-up from me."). No merge fields: the text is identical for every guest. Send the whole list 8 to 12 days before the session.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: Calendar invites are the main registration channel for this session; the live session walks through the full traffic stage. Readers who get our invite will recognise every line.
  • Source: context/from-tantra-content/05-funnel-systems.md §3.3.

BK2. Your LinkedIn Connection Note Fails in the First Line: Before and After

  • Format: Carousel, 10 pages. Sample HD33 (Harry Dry #33): red failed vs green working pairs with handwritten annotations, critique as content.
  • Hook: Your connection note pitches before they've accepted. That's why your acceptance rate falls every week. Five fixes, side by side.
  • Lesson: Five failed vs working pairs: (1) a note that pitches vs a specific detail, one line on the problem and one low-pressure question, inside 300 characters; (2) a first message with the event link vs a first message useful on its own; (3) the event link in message 1 vs a later message; (4) a request left hanging vs withdrawn at 14 days; (5) watching replies vs watching the acceptance rate, because a falling acceptance rate is the first sign the note or the list is wrong.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: LinkedIn outreach is one of the traffic channels the session takes apart; the first comment invites readers to watch the whole channel mix.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §3.2.

BK3. Five Emails, Thirteen Days: Audit Your Cold Email Thread

  • Format: Carousel, 8 pages. Sample HD15 (Harry Dry #15): dark art-directed pages, one email mockup per page ringed by hand-drawn self-audit questions.
  • Hook: Most cold email threads die at step 2 because step 2 repeats step 1. Hold yours against these five pages before you send another.
  • Lesson: The thread runs on days 0, 3, 6, 9 and 13: step 1 the promise, the event and one link; step 2 a short reply in the same thread with one new reason; step 3 one example from your own proof; step 4 the date again and the booking link; step 5 the last note with an easy out. Each page asks: one link only? An opt-out line? Readable on a phone? Would it read well if the company name were missing? A reply ends that person's thread.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session shows the email thread as one channel inside a six-stage system; the CTA invites the reader to see where the thread hands over to the reminders.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §3.1.

BK4. Stop Judging Posts by Likes: 5 Rules for Content That Fills a Session

  • Format: Carousel, 8 pages (cover, the steps, a close page; the engine minimum is 8). Sample AB02 (Austin Belcak #2): the fake-tweet card container, pure-text numbered steps, ultra minimal. Avatar needed (photos from Adi), or name line only (no photo).
  • Hook: A post with hundreds of likes and zero registrations didn't work. Here are 5 rules that turn content into a traffic channel.
  • Lesson: (1) Start from one pillar idea built on one real buyer pain. (2) Cut the rest from it: the problem, the method, the worked example, the mistake to avoid, the invite. (3) Before a dated session, post about it every 3 days. (4) The link lives in the first comment, tagged so each post counts its own sign-ups. (5) Judge each post by registrations first, then clicks, then impressions.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: This post is an example of the rule it teaches: it is one cut of the session's pillar idea, and its first comment is the invite.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §3.8.

BK5. Grow Outreach in Steps, Never in One Jump

  • Format: Carousel, 8 pages. Sample AB29 (Austin Belcak #29): editorial masthead cover, highlighter-box kickers, a "Start Here:" callout and a grey source line on every number.
  • Hook: 10,000 invites. Then 20,046. Then 30,000. Then 38,138. Four sessions, four steps up, never one jump. Here's how to climb.
  • Lesson: Scale volume in steps, each step only after the last run's numbers read clean, because a sudden jump in volume is the clearest signal a spam filter reads. Report registrations and calls, not RSVPs (episode 4 had 187 Yes and 221 Maybe; neither is a registration until the funnel counts it). The client's own record: 17 calls booked across episodes 1 to 3 (the post states the numbers and the rule; it does not claim why the client chose each step).
  • Proof: PROOF_PACK §3, FintechCircle: invites per episode (10,000; 20,046; 30,000; 38,138), episode 4 RSVPs (187 Yes, 221 Maybe), 17 calls booked in episodes 1 to 3. Client named once, contact not named.
  • Webinar tie: The session covers how volume is planned against inbox and sender capacity; the CTA is "see the capacity maths live".
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §3.3 (report registrations, not RSVPs) and §3.1 (scale in steps).

BK6. Three Paid Traffic Traps That Waste the Budget Before the Page Has Converted Anyone

  • Format: Carousel, 8 pages (cover, the steps, a close page; the engine minimum is 8). Sample PC50 (Patrick James Cumming #50): a vertical brand-colour cover panel, then one named trap per page on a tilted title tab with an annotated mock dashboard and an arrow.
  • Hook: Paid traffic into an unproven page buys you every leak in it. Three traps I'd check before you spend another dollar on ads.
  • Lesson: Trap 1: ads before the page has converted one registration from organic or outbound traffic. Trap 2: changing budget and creative in the same week, so you can't tell which moved the number; leave ads alone for several days. Trap 3: judging by clicks or cost per lead; rank every channel by cost per buyer each month and move budget to the cheapest, keeping a small test budget on the others.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session shows where ads sit in the channel order (last, after the page converts); the CTA invites founders who are about to switch ads on.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §3.7.

BK9. Run a LinkedIn Event Without Losing Your Registrations to LinkedIn

  • Format: Animated image, 1080 x 1350 loop (MP4, GIF fallback). Sample AV13 (Alex Vacca #13): seven stepped cards on a connector path, one colour per step, with a full-width table footer. Motion: the connector thread draws from step 1 to 7, each card glowing as it is reached.

Footer table: what to count (registrations in the CRM filtered to utm_source=linkedin) vs what to ignore (the event's attendee count).

BK10. Work Out What One Registration Is Worth Before You Run an Ad

  • Format: Animated image, 1080 x 1350 loop (MP4, GIF fallback). Sample AV17 (Alex Vacca #17): left-aligned headline, a glossy 3D object bleeding off the top right, four empty glass input rows that build; the founder cutout is replaced by the brand mark (no faces). Motion: the four rows fill one by one, then the result row glows.
  • Hook: You can't judge an ad by its cost per click. Three numbers you already own tell you what a registration is worth.
  • Lesson: Row 1: the most you would spend to win one buyer. Row 2: times your show rate. Row 3: times your close rate. Row 4: your target cost per registration. At or under target: add budget, not new variables. In between: hold and change exactly one thing. Well over, or no registrations after spending 3 times the target: pause that ad. If a rate is unknown, say so and measure it first.
  • Proof: None (a formula with the reader's own numbers; no example figures).
  • Webinar tie: The session ties every channel back to booked calls; the CTA invites readers to work their own target live.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §3.7.
