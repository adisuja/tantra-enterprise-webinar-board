Carousels

BR1. Your Main Domain Never Sends a Cold Email

  • Format: Carousel, 9 pages. Sample HD16 (Harry Dry #16): photoreal chrome 3D renders as the recurring motif (a chrome shield over the main domain, chrome spare domains beside it), then a pipeline slide and a numbered list. Kie.ai, no people.
  • Hook: One spam complaint wave on your main domain and your invoices, proposals and password resets start landing in junk too.
  • Lesson: Split your mail by risk. Invoices, proposals, password resets and replies to customers stay on your main domain; every cold email and cold invite goes from separate outreach domains. A flagged outreach domain then costs you a spare, not your brand. (How to name and keep those domains is BR3.)
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The live session's traffic stage runs on this setup; it has to be in place weeks before the first invite.
  • Source: context/from-tantra-content/05-funnel-systems.md §4 (main domain rule, domain naming, redirect).

BR2. Two SPF Records Break Everything, and Nothing Tells You

  • Format: Carousel, 8 pages. Sample HD40 (Harry Dry #40): surreal-metaphor cover (two keys jammed into one lock, liquid splash off the frame), then breakdown slides; the sample's weighted percentage list becomes "five records, each exactly once", since we have no sourced weights.
  • Hook: Your emails pass every test you run by eye. Then a second SPF record goes in, and authentication fails with no error message at all.
  • Lesson: Per outreach domain, each record exactly once: MX to Google; one SPF (v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all), edited, never added to; a 2048-bit DKIM key at google._domainkey; DMARC at _dmarc with reports to your own address; a 301 redirect. Never mix two MX sets.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session covers why outreach reaches inboxes or does not; this is the part founders skip.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §4 (DNS records, "two break authentication without any error message").

BR3. The Outreach Domain Quest: Four Levels, One Boss Called The Lapse

  • Format: Carousel, 10 pages. Sample HD22 (Harry Dry #22): retro handheld-game world with a named villain and ability cards; the sample's virality quest becomes an outreach-domain quest, one level per page, the final boss "The Lapse". Pixel art, no people.
  • Hook: An outreach domain takes weeks to earn its reputation and one missed renewal to lose it. Four levels to name it and keep it.
  • Lesson: Level 1, the name: a .com that reads as your brand at a glance (your brand plus try, get, hq, meet, team or join), no hyphens, never the main domain. Level 2, registration: one year, and decide auto-renew on purpose. Level 3, the senders: inboxes named after the real people who send. Level 4, the 301 redirect to your main site, so a curious prospect lands somewhere real. The boss: a warmed domain that lapses loses its reputation, so renewal is part of the build, not admin. (The DNS records are BR2 and BR8.)
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • New vs registry and this batch: the waiting clocks are the company page's TP5; this is naming, records and keeping the domain alive.
  • Webinar tie: The session's traffic stage starts on these domains; the CTA invites readers who want the full sending setup.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §4 (domain naming rules, registration and auto-renew, inboxes named after the people, 301 redirect).

BR4. Never Trust the Browser With a Payment

  • Format: Carousel, 8 pages. Sample HD27 (Harry Dry #27): full-bleed character slides with one colour grade and an orange verb highlight; the sample's cat becomes a non-human mascot (a brass sentinel robot) for character continuity. Kie.ai, no people.
  • Hook: A buyer lands on your "payment received" page. Did they pay? The page cannot tell you. Only one thing can.
  • Lesson: A payment counts only when the provider's signed webhook arrives. Check the signature, ignore a sale for another product, and count each event once even when the provider sends it twice. The thank-you page says "payment received" only after that check passes; until then it says the payment is being confirmed.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session's revenue stage wires payment to the pipeline; this is the rule that keeps the revenue number honest.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §13.3 (payment).

BR5. 4 Lines in Vendor Docs That Change How You Build a Funnel

  • Format: Carousel, 9 pages. Sample AB22 (Austin Belcak #22): minimal cover, then tweet-card blurbs each paired with a cover thumbnail; the book covers become plain document covers for each vendor page. Tweet header: avatar needed (photos from Adi), or name line only.
  • Hook: The rules that break funnels are rarely secret. They sit in vendor documentation nobody reads until something fails.
  • Lesson: Four lines and what each changes: Anthropic calls CLAUDE.md context, not enforced configuration (so controls live in code); a Bash deny rule is not a security boundary (so keys sit outside the agent's reach); Google organisations created on or after 3 May 2024 block service-account key creation until the owner overrides the policy (so plan that step); Apple Mail Privacy Protection (June 2021) makes opens unreliable (so judge by clicks and replies).
  • Proof: None (method only; each line cites its vendor source in the post).
  • Webinar tie: The session shows how an AI builds the system; these are the limits it is built around.
  • Source: 06-revops-ai-systems.md §5 (Anthropic docs lines); 05-funnel-systems.md §4 (Google policy date) and §15 item 1 (Apple MPP).

BR6. Stop Handing Your AI Agent Your Keys

  • Format: Carousel, 9 pages. Sample HD03 (Harry Dry #3): headline inside a stop sign on the cover, a winding-road flow slide, an iceberg slide; the road is the one door every call goes through, the iceberg holds the honest limits.
  • Hook: If your AI agent can read your API keys, it can spend your money. Most setups let it.
  • Lesson: Keys sit in one file the agent never reads (deny rules, a hook and a scan). Every vendor call goes through one script with a guarded table and a log. Then say the limits out loud: the gate covers calls through that script, not the network; say "the script refuses", never "impossible".
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session hands the build to AI; this is how you do it without handing over the bank account.
  • Source: 06-revops-ai-systems.md §5 (layers 4 and 6, honest limits).
  • New vs registry: T6 listed six guardrails for an agent running a funnel. This is only about keys and the one door, and it adds what T6 left out: the limits of the gate, stated honestly.

Animated Image Posts

BR7. One Admin Grant, Every Inbox: Test What It Can Actually Do

  • Format: Animated image, 1080 x 1350 loop (MP4, GIF fallback). Sample AV18 (Alex Vacca #18): radial hub with a central hexagon and six dot-routed branches into a card grid; the hexagon is the service account, the branches its six scopes. Motion: the permission branches light one by one; then the Gmail branches drop out and the inbox card flips to "Calendar only".
  • Hook: Your inboxes connected fine, but none of them can send email. One missing permission in Google Admin does that.
  • Lesson: In Google Workspace, an admin can grant a sending tool access to every inbox once (domain-wide delegation), so nobody signs in to each inbox. That grant lists permissions; miss the Gmail ones and every inbox connects for calendar invites only, with no error at connection time. Before warm-up starts, send one real email and one real calendar invite from each inbox.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session's calendar-invite channel depends on this connection being right.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §4 (inboxes, delegation, six-scope line).

BR8. Check What the World Sees: The DNS Test

  • Format: Animated image, 1080 x 1350 loop (MP4, GIF fallback). Sample AV22 (Alex Vacca #22): dark cover with a centred icon, wordmark line and a terminal window that builds; the sample's comment-keyword bar becomes the webinar line in the first comment (no lead magnet). Motion: the terminal types four public DNS lookups on an example domain and ticks each result.
  • Hook: Your DNS panel says the records are there. That is not what a receiving server sees. Check from the outside.
  • Lesson: Run public lookups, not your registrar's view. A pass: one v=spf1 record, a DKIM key, one v=DMARC1 record, MX pointing to Google, nothing listed twice. Any duplicate or missing line is fixed before a single cold email goes.
  • Proof: None (method only; the domain shown is a labelled example).
  • Webinar tie: The session's traffic stage assumes this check passed.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §4 ("check what the world sees with public DNS lookups").

BR9. Where Each Message Leaves From

  • Format: Animated image, 1080 x 1350 loop (MP4, GIF fallback). Sample AV50 (Alex Vacca #50): radial mind-map with a centre node, colour-coded hub chips and glowing curved connectors to uniform cards; hubs are sending sources, cards their rules. Motion: one hub (an outreach domain) turns red; every other hub stays green and keeps sending.
  • Hook: If one flag can stop your reminders, your invites and your cold email at once, you have built a single point of failure.
  • Lesson: Split the sources: the main domain sends no cold email; cold email from outreach domains; calendar invites from their own domain and Workspace; WhatsApp from the owner's own number with consent; SMS only for time-critical messages. A flag on one leaves the rest working.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session's nurture stage sends across all of these; this is the map behind it.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §3.3 (separate calendar domain), §3.4, §3.5, §4.

BR10. Capacity Grows by Adding Inboxes, Never by Pushing One Harder

  • Format: Animated image, 1200 x 627 landscape loop as the sample (MP4, GIF fallback). Sample AB28 (Austin Belcak #28): two columns, a checklist on the left and a realistic code-editor panel on the right holding the sending plan; byline as name only (no photo). Motion: a new inbox row is added and the capacity line rises; a slider pushing one inbox past 50 flashes red.
  • Hook: Need more sends this week? Raising one inbox's daily limit is the fastest way to lose it.
  • Lesson: Size from the plan: people, at least 2 domains, 2 or 3 inboxes per domain, 50 sends a day per warm inbox (20 while warming), weekly email capacity = inboxes x 50, LinkedIn capacity = people x 200 requests a week. A bigger list needs more inboxes or an earlier start. An inbox added later starts its own warm-up from zero.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session sizes the outreach for one live event; this is how the capacity behind it is planned.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §4 (sending plan) and §3.1 (limits).
