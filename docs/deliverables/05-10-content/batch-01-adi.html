AD1. Our First Webinar for a Client Had 7 People in the Room

  • Format: Carousel, 9 pages. Sample HD10 (Harry Dry #10): full-bleed photo with a white caption bubble anchored bottom-left, the raw "confession" story feel. Avatar poses: floor-overwhelmed (cover), headshot, speaking; 2 new poses needed (at a desk with a spreadsheet, looking at a phone).
  • Hook: 12,898 people reached. 30 registered. 7 came live. That was session one for a client in May 2025, and we booked the next one anyway.
  • Lesson: Judge a webinar programme over a series, never over one room, and switch on the instruments from session one: a weekly analytics sheet, an experiment log, a long and a short landing page tested against each other. Without them, session two is a guess.
  • Proof: PROOF_PACK §3, Growth Idea first webinar (6 May 2025: 12,898 reached, 30 registered, 7 live), "what we built" list, 11 webinars May 2025 to Feb 2026.
  • New vs registry: A4 and the unpicked I2 compared the best session's numbers; this one is about the decision to keep going and the instruments that made later sessions readable. No best-session numbers on the page.
  • Webinar tie: The live session opens with the same point: one session is a test of the message, a weekly series is the engine.
  • Source: context/from-tantra-content/05-funnel-systems.md §2.2 (webinar as the best first funnel, "one session shows which message lands"), §9 (experiment log).

AD2. 5 Roles, 3 Scripted Moments, 1 Dry Run: How I Run a Live Session

  • Format: Carousel, 10 pages. Sample HD24 (Harry Dry #24): protest-sign cover (avatar holding a sign reading "5 ROLES. 3 MOMENTS. 1 DRY RUN."), then kraft-paper pages with one numbered rule each. New pose needed (holding a sign).
  • Hook: The host should never be the one pasting links in the chat. A live session is five jobs, each with a real name next to it.

  • Proof: None (method only).

AD3. Why People Leave at Minute 10: The First 19 Minutes, Section by Section

  • Format: Carousel, 10 pages. Sample HD29 (Harry Dry #29): founder holding a notepad on the cover, then a dense calendar-style grid as a save-worthy resource. New pose needed (holding a notepad).

AD4. The Offer Goes in Once: 7 Rules for the 10 Minutes Before Q&A

  • Format: Carousel, 9 pages. Sample AB27 (Austin Belcak #27): dark editorial numbered list, avatar header on every page, grain texture, disciplined vertical rhythm. Avatar pose: headshot.
  • Hook: Say the price once. Show the next step four times. The 10 minutes before Q&A decide whether the session books calls.
  • Lesson: The bridge line before the offer; the offer sentence; what is included, then "How to Start" for a booked call instead of a price slide; the three questions everyone asks, answered on one slide; the call to action four times, the price once; the link in chat the moment a call-to-action slide goes up, then every five minutes; an empty answer removes the slide rather than padding it.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The reader can watch the move from teaching into the offer happen live, and judge whether it feels like a pitch.
  • Source: context/from-tantra-content/05-funnel-systems.md §2.2 (deck sections 7 to 10, price slide variants, design rules).
  • Optional story hook (only if Adi confirms it is true): "I used to repeat the price every time someone asked in the chat. Now it goes on screen once."

AD5. 3 Tests, 3 Targets Written Down Before Each One Ran

  • Format: Carousel, 8 pages. Sample AB47 (Austin Belcak #47): dark tweet-style pages with a repeated header band, result first, then a context page and numbered steps. Avatar pose: headshot (header chip).
  • Hook: We wrote the target down before every test. Target: 200 registrations. Result: 332. The target mattered more than the result.
  • Lesson: Before a run, write the one element you are changing, the number that decides it, and when you will read it. The three log rows: an event description change (target 200 registrations, got 332), a large calendar-invite push (target 250 attendees, got 441), a long landing page (target 70 registrations, got 96). A target set in advance is what turns a lucky week into a decision you can repeat.
  • Proof: PROOF_PACK §3, Growth Idea experiment log (indicative only; the post says so on the context page).
  • New vs registry: T5 taught finding the worst step by ratio; this teaches the pre-registered target, with a real log.
  • Webinar tie: The session shows how the weekly experiment loop runs after launch.
  • Source: context/from-tantra-content/05-funnel-systems.md §9 (experiment sizing, "name before the run").

AD8. 4 Polls Sort Your Room Before You Send a Single Follow-Up

  • Format: Animated image, 1080 x 1350 loop (MP4, GIF fallback). Sample AV23 (Alex Vacca #23): left-rail tiered pyramid, four tier cards with a three-part inner grid (what they said, what it means, what you do), a numbered row along the bottom.   • Hook: Your follow-up list is already sorted before the session ends. Four polls do it, if you read the answers.
