Carousel Threads

X1. A Booked Call Is Not Revenue

  • Source: TP1 (sample HD21). Thread of 6: the opening post, one post per place buyers fall out, a close.
  • Opening post: A booked call is not revenue. A proposal is not revenue. Money in the account is revenue. Buyers fall out in five places between the call and the payment. Here are all five, with the follow-up that catches each:
  • Lesson: One gap per post (no-show, no proposal, no answer, unpaid, never onboarded) and its follow-up.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: Self-reply: the registration link, "the pipeline after the call, live every week".

X2. Two SPF Records and Nothing Tells You

  • Source: BR2 (sample HD40). Thread of 6: one post per DNS record, each "exactly once".
  • Opening post: Add a second SPF record to your sending domain and authentication breaks without a single error message. Every record on an outreach domain should exist exactly once. The checklist:
  • Lesson: MX, one SPF (edit it, never add a second), DKIM, DMARC with reports to your own address, a 301 redirect; check from public DNS.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: Self-reply: the sending setup is the first build step in the session.

X3. One Generic Join Link and Your Follow-Up Is a Guess

  • Source: AQ2 (sample HD31). Thread of 5: the chain from registration to the right email.
  • Opening post: If every registrant joins your session through the same link, you can't tell who attended. Then attendees get the replay email and no-shows get the offer. One fix: a personal join link per person.
  • Lesson: Registration, personal link, join, attended tag, the right branch.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: Self-reply: every registrant of the session gets their own link, so readers see it working.

X4. Five Emails, Thirteen Days

  • Source: BK3 (sample HD15). Thread of 6: one post per step, then the audit questions.
  • Opening post: Most cold email threads die at step 2 because step 2 repeats step 1. Five emails over 13 days, each with one job. Hold yours against this before you send another:
  • Lesson: Days 0, 3, 6, 9, 13: promise and one link; one new reason; one proof example; the date and the booking link; an easy out. One link, an opt-out line, readable on a phone.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: Self-reply: the session shows every channel's sequence side by side.

X5. Is Your A/B Result Real? The 10-Second Check

  • Source: NE10 (sample AV33, re-rendered as 2 thread images). Thread of 4.
  • Opening post: Variant B got 15 more sign-ups than A. Before you rewrite every page: if the gap is smaller than 2 x the square root of (A + B), it's a handful of people. Run it again.
  • Lesson: The worked example, labelled made-up (60 of 500 against 75 of 500: gap 15, threshold about 23, not decided); never end a test early.
  • Proof: None (made-up example, labelled).
  • Webinar tie: Self-reply: the weekly test loop, shown live.

X6. Say the Price Once

  • Source: AD4 (sample AB27). Thread of 6: the 10 minutes before Q&A, one rule per post.
  • Opening post: On a live session, say the price once. Show the next step four times. Drop the link in the chat the moment the call-to-action slide goes up, then every five minutes. Seven rules for the 10 minutes before Q&A:
  • Lesson: The bridge line, the offer sentence, "How to Start" instead of a price slide for a booked call, the three questions everyone asks, the link cadence.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: Self-reply: watch the move into the offer happen live.

X9. What Is One Registration Worth?

  • Source: BK10 (sample AV17, input rows re-cut to 16:9). Motion: the four rows fill, the result glows.
  • Opening post: Don't judge an ad by cost per click. The most you'd pay for one buyer, times your show rate, times your close rate, is what one registration is worth. Then you know when to stop.
  • Lesson: At or under target: add budget. In between: change one thing. Well over, or nothing after 3 times the target: pause.
  • Proof: None (formula; the reader's own numbers).
  • Webinar tie: Self-reply: the session's traffic section, live.

X10. A Number You Couldn't Read Is Not a Zero

  • Source: NE9 (sample AV44, dashboard panels re-cut to 16:9). Motion: panels fill; one stays grey with "Not yet".
  • Opening post: A dashboard that shows 0 when it means "we couldn't fetch this" will send you chasing a problem that doesn't exist. Unread numbers say "not yet". Never zero, never estimated.
  • Lesson: One row per run and per source; test contacts out of every count; read a run only after it finishes.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: Self-reply: the dashboard read, live.
