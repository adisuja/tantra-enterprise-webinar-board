Carousels

TP1. A Booked Call Is Not Revenue. Neither Is a Proposal.

  • Format: Carousel, 8 pages. Sample HD21 (Harry Dry #21): strict two-row before/after pages with huge whitespace and the text-selection-handle device, top row "feels like revenue", bottom row "is revenue".
  • Hook: A booked call is not revenue. Nor is a proposal. Money in the account is revenue, and buyers quietly fall out in five places first.
  • Lesson: One page per gap between offer and money: booked but no-show, held but no proposal, proposal sent but no answer, accepted but unpaid, paid but never onboarded. Each page pairs the comfortable signal with the real one and names the follow-up that closes the gap (for example 15 minutes after a missed call, 48 hours after a proposal).
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session's last stage shows the pipeline after the call, where most funnels stop paying attention.
  • Source: context/from-tantra-content/05-funnel-systems.md §13.3 (the close, four sales follow-ups, stage matrix).

TP2. If a Deal Had to Be Moved by Hand, That Step Isn't Wired Yet

  • Format: Carousel, 9 pages. Sample HD02 (Harry Dry #2): modular 2x4 comparison grid with the editor drag-handle device, "moved by a person" read against "moved by an event".
  • Hook: If someone on your team drags deals between stages, your CRM is a diary, not a pipeline. Every stage move should be caused by an event.
  • Lesson: Tools report events and events move deals: a calendar booking moves the deal to Eval Call Scheduled; a cancellation moves it to Follow-Up; a no-show tags call-no-show; a finished meeting moves it to Sales Call Held; a confirmed payment marks it paid and starts onboarding; a full refund moves it back. Never rename a stage or a tag, because every sync matches on exact text.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session maps the pipeline stages a registrant moves through, from form to onboarding.
  • Source: context/from-tantra-content/05-funnel-systems.md §13.1 (stages and what enters each), §13.2 (sync rules).

TP3. Same Six Stages, Three Funnel Shapes: What Changes and What Doesn't

  • Format: Carousel, 10 pages. Sample HD13 (Harry Dry #13): marker-on-paper sketchbook, sticky-note columns and one hand-drawn table across the shapes.
  • Hook: A webinar, a recorded sales video and a three-day workshop look like three different funnels. Underneath, they run the same six stages.
  • Lesson: One sketchbook page per stage, three sticky notes per page (webinar, reverse squeeze, challenge): Capture is a registration page, a video page with no form up front, or a seat page; Nurture is reminders timed to a date, follow-ups set by watch depth, or a daily delivery loop; Conversion is the offer once in a live session, a second video, or the last day; Close and Growth are the same pipeline and the same year of follow-up for all three. The last page: what changes is the shape, never the stage.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • New vs registry: T2 taught how to pick a shape; T10 taught the four layers. This teaches what each stage looks like inside each shape.
  • Webinar tie: The session builds the webinar shape stage by stage.
  • Source: context/from-tantra-content/05-funnel-systems.md §1.2 (six stages, what each stage looks like per shape), §2.1.

TP4. The 52-Week Engine: What Happens to Everyone Who Didn't Buy

  • Format: Carousel, 9 pages. Sample HD35 (Harry Dry #35): paper-texture mind map radiating from a central node, then one checklist page per branch.
  • Hook: Most people at your session won't buy this week. The ones who buy in month four were kept warm by a system, not by luck.
  • Lesson: Branches: a welcome series of four weekly emails, each teaching one step of your method; then one letter a week on a 12-topic rotation (the problem, the mechanism, a customer story, a mistake, a tool, an invitation back in); about four useful sends for every one that asks; the same day and time every week; remove anyone after 20 unopened emails; any reply goes straight back into the pipeline; buyers leave the list.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session's last stage shows how the list after the session becomes next quarter's pipeline.
  • Source: context/from-tantra-content/05-funnel-systems.md §12 (the 52-week engine).

TP5. Start the Slow Clocks First: Warm the Domains, Then the Traffic

  • Format: Carousel, 8 pages. Sample HD28 (Harry Dry #28): one analogy object per page on matching white, a centred headline above each (a proving loaf for warm-up, a seedling for a new domain, a sealed envelope for the registry email, a wall calendar for the 60-day Workspace).
  • Hook: You can build a landing page in an afternoon. You can't rush a new sending domain. Start the slow clocks on day one.
  • Lesson: The clocks that cannot be rushed: confirm the registry email within 15 days or the domains are suspended; DNS can take up to 48 hours; outreach inboxes warm for at least 14 days before the first cold email; a calendar-invite Workspace needs 60 days to reach full volume. So the build order runs the other way round from instinct: domains first, pages and messages while they warm, traffic last.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session's build timeline starts with these clocks, so the reader knows what to start today.
  • Source: context/from-tantra-content/05-funnel-systems.md §4 (the chain and its waits), §2.2 (build order: "warm the domains first, traffic last").

TP6. Your Offer Scores Low on Two of Four Levers. Fix Those First.

  • Format: Carousel, 8 pages. Sample PC36 (Patrick James Cumming #36): four numbered quadrant cards on the cover, then one lever per page with a short diagnostic list, fixed in order.
  • Hook: When a session gets a full room and no calls, the deck gets the blame. Usually the offer scores low on two of four levers.
  • Lesson: The value equation (Alex Hormozi, $100M Offers): value = dream outcome x likelihood of getting it, divided by time delay x effort. Score each lever out of 10 as hard as a sceptical buyer would, then fix only the two lowest before changing a slide.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session's offer section is built from this score.
  • Source: context/from-tantra-content/04-workshop-curriculum.md §5.2 (value equation, card D1·06).

Animated Image Posts

TP7. Every Funnel Does Four Jobs. Only the Shape Changes.

  • Format: Animated image, 1080 x 1350 loop (MP4, GIF fallback). Sample AV07 (Alex Vacca #7): radial hub and spoke, a central node with a dotted ring and colour-coded cards orbiting it. Motion: connectors light in turn from the hub to traffic, capture, convert, retain, then the three shape labels fade in on each card.
  • Hook: Traffic, capture, convert, retain. Every funnel you've ever seen does these four jobs. Only the shape changes.
  • Lesson: Each job has one test you can run this week: traffic (is every link tagged by source?), capture (does the page earn the click?), convert (is there a door open at every stage, not only at the end?), retain (does everyone who didn't buy stay warm for a year?).
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session opens on this map before going stage by stage.
  • Source: context/from-tantra-content/05-funnel-systems.md §1.4 (four jobs, four mechanisms).

TP8. Booked Call or Checkout? Two Questions Decide How You Close.

  • Format: Animated image, 1080 x 1350 loop (MP4, GIF fallback). Sample AV25 (Alex Vacca #25): 2x2 matrix with rotated axis labels and two yes/no question cards on top; each quadrant carries a play and a failure mode. Motion: the two answers toggle and the matching quadrant lights.
  • Hook: Some offers should never need a sales call. Others should never go to a checkout page. Two questions tell you which yours is.
  • Lesson: Question 1: is the price around $1,000 or more? Question 2: does the buyer need screening? The four closes: direct checkout (the page answers every objection); checkout below a price and a call above it; booked call, then proposal and payment; application, then a call. Failure mode per quadrant (for example, a high price on a checkout page with no one to answer the last objection).
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session shows the close a high-ticket offer needs, and why the call is booked during the session.
  • Source: context/from-tantra-content/05-funnel-systems.md §13.3 (close path by price).

TP9. The First 48 Hours After Payment Decide Who Stays

  • Format: Animated image, 1080 x 1350 loop (MP4, GIF fallback). Sample PC24 (Patrick James Cumming #24): three phase columns of numbered step cards linked by arrows over a progress rail; the last card floods with the accent colour. Motion: the rail fills from "payment clears" to "first win", each card lighting as it is reached.
  • Hook: Customers don't leave the moment they pay. They leave in the gap between paying you and their first result.
  • Lesson: Three phases: the moment payment clears (the welcome email goes at once and the deal moves to Onboarding); the first 48 hours (the kickoff is booked, for a date within 3 to 7 business days); the first win (written in the buyer's own words, with a date). People buy on a promise and stay on a result.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session's pipeline stage ends at onboarding, not at payment.
  • Source: context/from-tantra-content/05-funnel-systems.md §11 (why retention), §13.3 (stage matrix: paid).

TP10. Stop Adding Channels Until These 5 Lines Exist

  • Format: Animated image, 1080 x 1350 loop (MP4, GIF fallback). Sample AV46 (Alex Vacca #46): a five-tier pyramid with labels rotated along both slopes and two side panels. Motion: the tiers build from the base up, one line per tier.
  • Hook: Adding a channel feels like progress. Without these 5 lines, written monthly, it just moves the leak somewhere new.
  • Lesson: The monthly scale plan: (1) cost per buyer and lifetime value, both fetched, never estimated (lifetime value around three times cost per buyer is healthy); (2) the cheapest channel by cost per buyer, never cost per click; (3) the worst step and this week's experiment; (4) the next offer up the ladder, with its price; (5) the retention mechanism: the first win, by when.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session ends on how the weekly and monthly numbers decide where the next pound or dollar goes.
  • Source: context/from-tantra-content/05-funnel-systems.md §11 (the scale plan).
