Carousels

NE1. Without a UTM, You Cannot Defend the Budget

  • Format: Carousel, 9 pages. Sample HD37 (Harry Dry #37): concept infographic with per-word colour highlighting; each part of one tagged link gets its own colour, with a paint-roller edge on the cover.
  • Hook: Someone asks which channel earned last month's registrations. If your links carry no tags, the honest answer is "no idea".
  • Lesson: One tagged link per channel, read part by part: source (email, calendar, linkedin, meta, blog), medium (cold, invite, social, paid, organic), campaign (the event slug), content (the post or variant). Never type a tagged link by hand, and judge each channel by booked calls and revenue, not clicks.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The live session shows the traffic stage with every channel on its own tagged row; this is the first thing to set up before any of it runs.
  • Source: context/from-tantra-content/05-funnel-systems.md §14 (UTM convention per channel, the playbook hand recipe).

NE2. The Weekly Review Hour, Step by Step

  • Format: Carousel, 10 pages. Sample AB33 (Austin Belcak #33): dark numbered slides; step 1 in a green rounded callout with a marker-style checklist; multi-hue highlight boxes. Name line on each slide: avatar needed (photos from Adi), or name only.
  • Hook: Most funnels are reviewed when something breaks. Here is the one hour a week that stops it breaking quietly.
  • Lesson: Eight steps, same hour every week: post the outside numbers to the dashboard; run the channel check and name the worst step; close last week's experiment (keep or revert, log it); open one new experiment; check every automation still reads "proven"; monthly, rewrite the scale plan; write a short report; when a run ends, schedule the next event and re-invite the nurture list.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session's last stage is running the engine week after week; this is the routine it hands you.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §10 (weekly review, operate.md 129 to 144).
  • New vs registry: T4 taught the three thresholds of the channel check; this teaches the whole hour around it, with the check as one step of eight.

NE3. Two Clocks Most Teams Never Set: the Enquiry and the Invoice

  • Format: Carousel, 9 pages. Sample AB41 (Austin Belcak #41): dark numbered slides with italic fill-in-the-blank script blocks; here each block is a trigger line, When [event], do [action] within [time]. Header band: avatar needed (photos from Adi), or name only.
  • Hook: A buyer asks for a call at 10:02. If nobody calls until tomorrow, you are now the second company they spoke to.
  • Lesson: The enquiry clock: the booking link goes at once, a call within 5 minutes in working hours, then a call a day for 3 days. The invoice clock: reminders 3 days before it is due, on the day, then 3 and 7 days late. Each sequence has one exit rule: a reply, a booking or a payment ends it. (The follow-ups between the call and the payment are the company page's TP1.)
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session ends on the call to action; this is what has to be ready the minute someone acts on it.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §13.3 (stage matrix, sales.md 62 to 79).

NE4. Same Offer, Two Packages: Tag Every Variant

  • Format: Carousel, 8 pages. Sample HD46 (Harry Dry #46): physical-packaging metaphor carried across slides (one product, a plain box and a good box) with cross and tick badges; the boxes become two headline variants of one page.
  • Hook: You tested two headlines. Both sent traffic to the same link. So which one won? You cannot know.
  • Lesson: Every variant gets its own utm_content (variant_a, variant_b), so the dashboard counts separate rows. Headline tests store the variant on the contact even when the link carried none. Split the list in half, one campaign per half; without the split, two tests read as one blended number.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The live session covers testing one element a week; this is the tagging that makes the result readable.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §9 (experiment split) and §14 (headline test row, landing_variant).

NE5. 6 List Rules That Keep You Out of the Spam Folder and Out of Trouble

  • Format: Carousel, 9 pages. Sample AB48 (Austin Belcak #48): light numbered-principle slides, one column of text, imagery on the cover only; the cover illustration swaps the sample's figure for objects (a ledger and a stamp), so no face is needed.
  • Hook: One person who asked to stop and got messaged again can cost you more than the whole campaign earned.
  • Lesson: WhatsApp only to people who ticked the box, with "Reply STOP" in every message; anyone who asks to stop goes on the do-not-contact list that day; one person per row; never delete or reorder rows in the master sheet; every row has a work email and a LinkedIn URL; reject a bought file with more than 2% duplicates.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session's traffic stage starts from a list; these rules decide whether that list is an asset or a liability.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §3.4 (consent, STOP), §3.2 (do-not-contact), §5 (master sheet, duplicates).

NE6. Anatomy of a Measured Run: Every Rate as a Count Over a Count

  • Format: Carousel, 9 pages. Sample HD32 (Harry Dry #32): dark-cyber grade with phone mockups held in a rendered hand showing example slides; no-face treatment (the cover is the hand and the phone, not a portrait).
  • Hook: "Our webinar converted well" is not a number. Here is what one client's run looks like when every rate shows its working.
  • Lesson: Write each rate with its numerator and denominator, across the whole series of 11 webinars. Registered: 6,517 of 765,105 reached (0.85%). Came live: 1,384 of 6,517 (21.2%). Sales calls: 80 of 1,384 live (5.8%). Clients: 4 of 80 calls (5%). Each step is one count over the one before, so anyone can check it, and the smallest ratio tells you where to look first.
  • Proof: PROOF_PACK §3, Growth Idea series totals (11 webinars, May 2025 to Feb 2026: 765,105 reached, 6,517 registered, 1,384 live, 80 sales calls, 4 clients). The four percentages are arithmetic on those counts, shown with their working. Name the client once.
  • Webinar tie: The live session teaches the funnel equation stage by stage; this shows it on real counts.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §8 step 1 (write each metric as numerator over denominator); PROOF_PACK §3.
  • New vs registry: A4 used the best session's counts; AD1 uses the first webinar's. This uses only the series totals, as count-over-count rates, to teach how to measure; no claims about causes.

Animated Image Posts

NE7. One Owner, Four Clocks

  • Format: Animated image, 1080 x 1350 loop (MP4, GIF fallback). Sample AV15 (Alex Vacca #15): true org-chart tree with annotation tabs and a stat-card row; the owner node branches into four clock nodes, the stat cards become "what you read". Motion: the four branches draw in one after another, each card ticking on its own clock.
  • Hook: Checking the numbers every hour is as bad as checking them every quarter. Each decision has its own clock.
  • Lesson: Refresh the dashboard daily. Run one experiment a week. Rebalance budget monthly, toward the cheapest channel by cost per buyer. Revisit the offer and the buyer persona quarterly. Mixing the clocks is how teams end a test early or move budget on one bad day.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session ends with how the engine is run after launch; this is the rhythm it runs on.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §9 (cadence) and §11 (monthly scale plan).

NE8. Every Registrant Gets Their Own Share Link

  • Format: Animated image, 1080 x 1350 loop (MP4, GIF fallback). Sample AV29 (Alex Vacca #29): three device frames in perspective with name-tag chips pinned to each; no faces (the chips carry example tags, labelled as examples, not people). Motion: a tag chip slides off each device into one dashboard row, which counts per tag.
  • Hook: Your best traffic source might be the people who already registered. You only find out if each of them has their own link.
  • Lesson: Give every registrant a share link carrying their own tag, plus a ready-made post to share. Each sign-up through it is credited to the person who shared, so you can see who brings people and thank or reward them by name.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session shows the registration and thank-you pages; the share link belongs on the second one.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §2.4 (participants recruit participants, own-tag share links) and §3.9 (referrals).

NE9. A Number You Could Not Read Is Not a Zero

  • Format: Animated image, 1080 x 1350 loop (MP4, GIF fallback). Sample AV44 (Alex Vacca #44): eight bordered dashboard panels with a per-module colour border, a real comparison table and a red-flag list. Motion: panels fill one by one; one panel stays grey with a "Not yet" chip instead of flashing 0.
  • Hook: A dashboard that shows 0 when it means "we could not fetch this" will send you chasing a problem that does not exist.
  • Lesson: A number you could not read stays out ("not yet"), never zero, never estimated. One row per run and per source, because a strong channel hides a weak one in the average. Test contacts stay out of every count. Read a run only after it has finished.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: The session's measurement stage uses this dashboard; these are the rules that make it trustworthy.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §14 (dashboard rules) and §6.2 (test contacts excluded, a blank is information).

NE10. Is Your A/B Result Real? The 10-Second Check

  • Format: Animated image, 1080 x 1350 loop (MP4, GIF fallback). Sample AV33 (Alex Vacca #33): 2x2 diagnostic grid, four different chart types (a pyramid, a matrix, twin donut gauges, a stepped ladder) under numbered tabs. Motion: the two donut gauges fill, then a stamp lands on the ladder: "run it again".
  • Hook: Variant B got 15 more sign-ups than A. Before you rewrite every page, run this check.
  • Lesson: Name the element, the deciding number and the read date before the run. When it ends, if the gap between the two counts is smaller than 2 x the square root of (A + B), it is a handful of people: run the same test again. Worked example, made-up numbers: 60 of 500 against 75 of 500 is a gap of 15; 2 x the square root of 135 is about 23, so not decided. Never end a test early because one half looks ahead after a day.
  • Proof: None (method only; the example is labelled as made up).
  • Webinar tie: The session's last stage is one controlled experiment a week; this keeps it from fooling you.
  • Source: 05-funnel-systems.md §9 steps 1 and 4 (TEACHING MODEL illustration, labelled).
  • New vs registry: T5 taught finding the one step to fix; this teaches judging whether a fix worked, with the noise check it did not cover.
