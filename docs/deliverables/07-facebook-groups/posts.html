1. Founder and Entrepreneur Groups

Typical rules: no direct selling, value posts welcome, links often removed. Default: first comment.

Quick question for the founders here: could you say today where next month's revenue will come from?

If the honest answer is "referrals, and me", you're running on the two least predictable channels there are.

Here's the frame my team uses instead:

1. Treat revenue as six steps, not one: who you reach, who signs up, who gets reminded, who shows up, who books a call, who buys.
2. Measure each step on its own. A bad month nearly always has one step that broke.
3. Fix one step at a time. A small lift near the top carries all the way down.
4. Hand the repetitive work (invites, reminders, follow-ups) to AI. Keep the conversations for yourself.

Step 2 is the one most founders skip, and it's the one that tells you what to fix.

2. B2B Sales and Marketing Groups

Typical rules: tactics welcome, no vendor pitches, links in comments. Default: first comment.

The most underrated B2B channel I've worked with isn't LinkedIn or cold email. It's the calendar invite.

You send the event straight to the prospect's calendar. No email to open, no page to find. They see a title and a time, and they answer Yes, Maybe or No.

One example. Growth Idea, a UK business-coaching group, got 30 registrations on its first webinar. On its best one it got 1,990, and 98,000 of the 99,859 people it reached that week came through calendar invites.

What made it work:

1. Separate domains and a separate Google Workspace for the invites. Never your main domain.
2. A slow start: 20 invites a day per sender for the first 60 days, then up to 50.
3. A title that reads like a meeting. No "$", no "%", no "free".
4. Date, time and time zone in the first line of the description.
5. One line that makes declining easy. People trust an invite that lets them say no.

3. Agency Owner Groups

Typical rules: no client poaching, no service ads, promo days in some. Default: first comment, or the promo thread where one exists.

Agency owners: how much of last quarter's new business came from referrals?

For many agencies it's most of it. That's fine until two clients leave in the same month.

The fix that works is the boring one: build your own new-business funnel before you build one for a client. In this order:

1. Warm the sending domains first. They need weeks before they can carry volume.
2. Build the registration page and the reminders next.
3. Then the session itself and the call booking that follows it.
4. Switch the traffic on last.

Never send traffic into a half-built funnel. It burns the list, and a burned list doesn't come back.

A London fintech network we built this for grew its outreach from 10,000 to 30,000 people across three sessions and booked 17 sales calls along the way.

4. Coaching and Consulting Groups

Typical rules: no "DM me" offers, frameworks welcome, admin approval common. Default: first comment.

Coaches and consultants: you're probably great once you're on a call. The hard part is getting enough of the right people onto one.

Here's the maths I use to plan a webinar that fills a sales calendar. It runs backward from the result.

A UK coaching company my team worked with had this on its best webinar: 1,990 registered, 321 came live, 25 sales calls booked. That's about 16% showing up, and about 8 calls for every 100 people live.

So if you want 25 sales calls from one session:
• you need about 310 people live (25 calls at 8 per 100)
• which means about 1,950 registrations (310 at a 16% show-up)

That second number tells you how much outreach you need before you write a word of copy. Most people plan the other way round and wonder why the calendar stays empty.

Run your own numbers through it. If they don't add up, the fix is usually at the top (reach) or the middle (show-up), not the pitch.

5. SaaS Groups

Typical rules: no product launches outside a launch thread, technical value welcome. Default: first comment.

SaaS founders: if your demo calendar is thin, check how many registrants turn up before you rewrite your ads.

Show-up is the cheapest step in the funnel to fix. Those people already said yes, and you've already paid to reach them.

What my team sets up on every webinar funnel:

1. Reminders a week before, the day before, an hour before and 15 minutes before.
2. Two channels, email and WhatsApp, so one missed inbox doesn't cost you the seat.
3. Attendance tracked per person, so attendees get the offer and no-shows get the recording.
4. A follow-up the same day, while the session is still fresh.

Every point you add to show-up flows straight down into booked demos.

6. Regional Business Groups

Typical rules: local members, community tone, promotion often limited to one day a week. Default: the promo thread or day where one exists, else first comment. Fill {{region}} (for example "Bengaluru").

To the business owners in {{region}}: a simple test for next month's revenue.

Write down three numbers from last month:
1. How many new people heard from you
2. How many of them agreed to a conversation
3. How many of those conversations turned into paying clients

If you can't fill in all three, that's the first fix, ahead of any new tool or new hire. You can't improve a step you can't see.

If you can, work out the two rates between them: 2 divided by 1, then 3 divided by 2. The lower one is the step to fix first.

My team has worked with 250+ brands across 21+ countries, and every build starts with these three numbers.
