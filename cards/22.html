Headline: The 20-Minute Review I'd Run Every Week

Hi {{first_name}},

Most funnels don't break in one go. They drift. A reminder stops sending, a page gets slower, a follow-up goes out a day late, and nobody notices for a month.

So block 20 minutes a week, same day, same time:

1. Look at three numbers: registration to live, live to calls, calls to sales.
2. Find the one that moved the wrong way since last week.
3. Change one thing to fix it, and write down what you changed.

That's it. One change a week adds up faster than a big rebuild twice a year. I walk through how I read these numbers in the recording.

Subject: Why most registrants never show up Preview text: On one client's biggest webinar, about 16 in 100 came live. Here's the fix.

Why Most of Your Registrants Never Show Up

Hi {{first_name}},

The Leak

You spend weeks filling a webinar. The registration count looks great. Then you go live, and the room is a fraction of the list.

It's not just you. On one client's biggest webinar with us, about 16 of every 100 registrants came live.

Most founders treat that as a fact of life. I treat it as the biggest leak in the funnel, because every person who doesn't show up is a person you already paid to reach.

The Fix: A Reminder Ladder Timed From the Start

Most reminder sequences are timed from the moment someone registers. That breaks as soon as you run weekly, because people register on any day of the week.

So time everything from the start of the session instead:

  1. Confirmation, at once. Date, time, personal join link, and one ask: put it in your calendar.
  2. A reason to come, during the week. Short videos that each teach one piece of what's coming. People show up for things they've already started.
  3. The day before. Ask them to bring something: their numbers, one question. A reminder becomes a commitment.
  4. Three hours, one hour, fifteen minutes. Short, one link each. On WhatsApp too, for anyone who opted in.
  5. Live now, and ten minutes late. "We started 10 minutes ago, join now" brings back people who meant to come and lost track of time.

Two rules make it work for late registrants. Skip any step whose time has already passed. And never send two messages within 30 minutes of each other.

The Proof

Growth Idea, a UK business-coaching group, ran 11 webinars with us between May 2025 and February 2026. Across them, 6,517 people registered and 1,384 came live.

That gap between registered and live is the leak this ladder exists to close.

Do This This Week

  • Open your last webinar's numbers. Divide live attendees by registrants.
  • List every message a registrant got between signing up and the start.
  • Find the longest silence. That's where your first new reminder goes.

Run Every Webinar as an Experiment

The Leak

Most teams run webinar number five exactly like webinar number one, then wonder why the numbers don't move.

Or they change six things at once, the numbers move, and nobody can say which change did it. So they can't repeat it.

The Fix: One Experiment Log, One Weekly Sheet

  1. Before each webinar, write down one change and one target. "New event description. Target: 200 registrations."
  2. Change only that one thing.
  3. After the webinar, write the real number next to the target.
  4. Keep what beat its target. Drop what didn't.
  5. Review one weekly sheet: reach, registrations, live attendees, calls. The weakest number picks next week's experiment.

It sounds slow. It's the fastest way I know to get better every single week.
