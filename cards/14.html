2-SURVEY.md

The survey: ten questions that build the Revenue Plan

Round 2 (Adi 2026-11-08): Typeform-style, with an icon on every option.

How it works

  • One question per screen. Each question has big type, its number and an arrow, and a short help line where needed.
  • Choices.
  • Each option is a row with a letter key (A, B, C), an icon and the label; a picked option shows a tick.
  • A single choice blinks and moves on by itself. Multi-picks and amounts use OK; press Enter to continue.
  • Letter keys pick options, even before the survey has focus.
  • Amounts. Typed into a big underlined field with a dollar icon. Placeholders are dimmed; the entry offer shows a dimmed 0.
  • Moving around.
  • Each question slides up to the next.
  • Up and down arrows bottom right go back and forth, with "Question n of 10" beside them.
  • A thin progress line runs along the top.
  • Errors. "Please make a selection" or "Please enter an amount", with a short shake.
  • Icons.
  • Answers that are levels (revenue bands, years, clients, case studies, brands, hours, people) use a level meter: the higher the answer, the more bars lit.
  • Every other option has its own line icon.

3-REVENUE-PLAN-MODEL.md

The Revenue Plan model: ten funnels, six stages, every KPI

Generated from the calculator's own code (src/assets/plan-model.js in the pages folder) by tools/model-doc.mjs. Change the code, then rerun it. Round 2 (Adi 2026-11-08): every rate is built on our own webinar data (7-OUR-WEBINAR-DATA.md) and set conservatively.

How the plan is worked out

  1. Goal. The 12-month revenue goal from the survey (blank: new revenue equal to today's yearly run rate, at least $120,000). On the plan it is the big number on the right, and it can be typed over.
  2. Revenue per sign-up. Each funnel is a chain of stage rates from a sign-up (registrant, lead, application, half-watcher, member, commenter) to a paying customer. One sign-up's expected revenue is the chain's customers times the revenue per customer in the first 12 months, plus any low-ticket sales (OOO and SLO).
  3. Sign-ups needed a month. The goal divided by the revenue per sign-up, spread over the funnel's cycles (52 webinars a year, 6 challenges, 3 launches, otherwise 12 months).
  4. Traffic. Your own audience first, at a warm rate (2.5 times the cold page rate). The rest is split across the channels picked in the survey: the ad budget buys what it can, then calendar invites, cold email, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, content and partners share the remainder by weight (70, 10, 10, 5, 5, 5), or by the shares typed on the plan. Event funnels register people straight from a channel; the others send people to a page first. The plan shows how many invites, emails, connection requests and messages to send, and the ad spend.
  5. Revenue per customer. One-time and annual offers: the price. Monthly offers: customers join evenly through the year and leave at the monthly churn (9% for a cold start down to 4% for the strongest profile), so the average customer pays for fewer than 12 months.
  6. Personalisation. The survey answers place each KPI between 10% and 80% of the way from low to strong: conservative by design, never the top of a range. Close and buy-online rates move with the price (the whole range scales). Every KPI can be typed over on the plan; the arrow puts it back.
  7. The next 52 weeks. A small line under the plan: extra customers from a year of nurture for the people who did not buy, never counted in the goal.

Funnel fit (question 10)

Every funnel starts at 50 points; the answers add or take away points with a reason the person sees (price, sales motion, B2B or B2C, audience size, ad budget, hours, proof, funnels already built). How the other nine funnels are extrapolated

The public webinar benchmarks (6-BENCHMARKS.md) sit well above our own numbers: cold registration pages convert about a fifth of the published 10% to 30%, show-up runs about 40% of the published 44% to 58%, and booking runs about 70% of the planning figures used before. Each other funnel's public benchmark, or its Funnel Master diagram figure, is scaled down by the same ratio for the matching stage: page and opt-in rates by about a fifth, attendance and nurture rates by about half, booking and purchase rates by about two thirds. The close rate is shared by every call funnel, with the same price bands; book a call uses 0.8 times it (colder calls, no session before them) and social following 1.2 times (warm DM conversations).

Traffic channels: event funnels (webinar, workshop, challenge, launch) register people straight from the calendar invite, LinkedIn message, email reply or WhatsApp message, as our funnels do. Every other funnel sends the link to a page, and the page rate applies. Your own email list and followers convert at 2.5 times the cold page rate (public benchmark: warm traffic converts 2 to 3 times cold). The default channel mix follows how we run: calendar invites carry most of the volume, the ad budget buys what it can, and the remaining channels share the rest.
