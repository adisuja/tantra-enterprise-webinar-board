Batch 08: Facebook (Adi's Profile), Repurposed: 10 Ideas to Pick From

Stop here. Nothing is drafted or rendered until Adi picks 5: 3 carousels (FB1 to FB6) and 2 animated image posts (FB7 to FB10).

  • What repurposed means here: each idea is one of this funnel's LinkedIn ideas, rewritten for Adi's Facebook friends and followers. It keeps its source's lesson, proof and taxonomy sample. If Adi also picks the source on LinkedIn, the Facebook post is built from those finished assets (cheapest); if not, it is built fresh for Facebook from the same sample.
  • Voice: I/you, British English, warmer and more personal than LinkedIn: the story first, the lesson second. Company-page sources are rewritten in Adi's own voice.
  • Format on Facebook: a carousel becomes a multi-photo post of the same 1080 x 1350 pages (Facebook shows the first few in a grid, so page 1 and 2 must carry the hook); an animated image is uploaded as a short MP4 loop. Re-check Meta's current image and video limits before the build.
  • CTA: the registration link goes in the first comment (utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=enterprise-webinar&utm_content=<ID>), never in the post body. The post teaches; it never names the offer or its price.
  • Avatar: images of Adi use his Kie.ai avatar only (same poses as the source idea).

Carousels

FB1. Seven People Came to Our First Client Webinar. We Ran Ten More.

  • Source: AD1 (sample HD10, full-bleed photo with caption bubbles).
  • Format: Multi-photo post, 9 images.
  • Hook: Our first webinar for a client: 12,898 people reached, 30 signed up, 7 turned up. Here's why we booked the next one anyway.
  • Lesson: Judge a webinar programme over a series, and switch on the instruments (a weekly numbers sheet, an experiment log, two landing pages tested against each other) from the first session.
  • Proof: PROOF_PACK §3, Growth Idea first webinar (6 May 2025) and the 11-webinar run. Client named once.
  • Webinar tie: First comment: "I walk through the whole series model live every week."

FB2. The Founder Who Never Applied Because He Had No Slides

  • Source: AD6 (sample AB50, dark tweet-style story pages).
  • Format: Multi-photo post, 8 images.
  • Hook: He'd spent a year researching his business. He never applied to the event because he didn't have a slide deck. I didn't tell him the truth.
  • Lesson: Pick the date before the deck. A dated event forces the build; a plan without a date never ships. Five rules for a first session, ending on "Just friggin START."
  • Proof: None. Story from Adi's own post (voice sample Post 5, BL-02).
  • Webinar tie: First comment: the weekly live session as the reader's dated first step.

FB3. Five People, One Live Session: Who Does What

  • Source: AD2 (sample HD24, sign-holding cover plus kraft-paper rules).
  • Format: Multi-photo post, 10 images.
  • Hook: If you're hosting and pasting links in the chat at the same time, you're doing two jobs badly. A live session is five jobs.
  • Lesson: Five named roles (host, content moderator, tech moderator, chat and Q&A, DM operator), three moments said word for word, a chat question every 8 to 10 minutes, a dry run 2 to 3 days before on the same machine.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: First comment: "Watch the roles work from the attendee's seat."

FB4. Write the Target Down Before the Test

  • Source: AD5 (sample AB47, dark tweet-style pages, result first).
  • Format: Multi-photo post, 8 images.
  • Hook: We wrote a target down before every test. Target 200 sign-ups, result 332. The habit mattered more than the result.
  • Lesson: Before a run, write the one thing you're changing, the number that decides it and when you'll read it. Three real log rows, labelled indicative.
  • Proof: PROOF_PACK §3, Growth Idea experiment log (indicative only).
  • Webinar tie: First comment: the weekly test loop, shown live.

FB5. A Booked Call Isn't Money in the Bank

  • Source: TP1 (sample HD21, two-row before/after pages), rewritten in Adi's voice.
  • Format: Multi-photo post, 8 images.
  • Hook: A booked call isn't money in the bank. Buyers slip away in five places between the call and the payment.
  • Lesson: One page per gap between the offer and the money (no-show, no proposal, no answer, unpaid, never onboarded), each with the follow-up that closes it.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Optional story hook (only if Adi confirms it is true): "I used to celebrate booked calls. Then I counted how many turned into money."
  • Webinar tie: First comment: the pipeline after the call, walked through live.

FB6. What Happens to Everyone Who Didn't Buy

  • Source: TP4 (sample HD35, mind map then checklist pages), rewritten in Adi's voice.
  • Format: Multi-photo post, 9 images.
  • Hook: Most people who come to a session won't buy that week. The ones who buy four months later were kept warm on purpose.
  • Lesson: Four welcome emails, then one letter a week on a 12-topic rotation; about four useful sends for every one that asks; the same day every week; any reply goes straight back to a person.
  • Proof: None (method only).
  • Webinar tie: First comment: the year-long follow-up, shown in the session's last stage.
