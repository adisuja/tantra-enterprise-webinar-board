ab-test-plan.md

A/B Test Plan: Calendar Invite A vs B

What Is Tested

The invite as a whole: A (meeting-style title "AI revenue generation teardown (60 min)", no merge fields) against B (problem title "Founder Pipeline Teardown: Where AI Fits", a problem-led opening and two Tantra merge tags: "Hi {{firstName}}," and "the pipeline at {{company}}"; no AI personalisation in either). Everything else is identical: tail copy, link, sender pool, send days and hours, session.

Baseline (from references/cold-calendar-exports-E150-E162-stats.md)

Number Value How
Yes/Maybe rate, pooled 0.73% 18,933 Yes/Maybe from 2,610,407 invites (E150 to E162; E161 Pt.1 left out, only its 1-hour list survives)
Yes/Maybe range 0.28% to 1.66% per event and sender pool
Sender pool effect Pt.1 median 0.97%, Pt.2 median 0.43% the sender pool moved the rate more than 2x
No rate, pooled 6.0% same rows
Registrations per person reached (reference) 0.23% to 2.0% Growth Idea, first and best webinar (PROOF_PACK.md); the exports hold no registration counts

The exports carry no event titles, so no past title can be credited with its rate. This test starts that record.

Split

  • 50/50, inside every sender: each calendar sender sends half its slice as A and half as B (list_split in calendar-import.csv). The pool effect above would swamp a copy effect if A and B went out from different senders.
  • Within a sender's slice, assign by a hash of the lowercased email (even: A, odd: B), so the split is random and repeatable.
  • Same list filters for both (item 2: no junk, no role addresses, no past decliners).
  • Both campaigns launch the same day and run the same days and hours.

Sample Size

40,000 invites per variant, 80,000 in total, inside one week's 100,000 target.

At a 0.73% Yes/Maybe baseline, 38,637 invites per variant detect a 25% relative lift (0.73% to 0.91%) at 95% confidence and 80% power: n = (1.96 + 0.84)^2 x (p1(1 - p1) + p2(1 - p2)) / (p2 - p1)^2. At registration rates of 2.0%, the same size detects a 15% lift; at 0.5%, a 30% lift.

Metrics

Metric Definition Source
Registration rate (primary) registrations with utm_content=cal_a (or cal_b) divided by invites sent CRM Webinar tab (webinar.registered, utm_content)
Yes/Maybe rate (secondary) Yes + Maybe RSVPs divided by invites sent Tantra campaign stats
No rate (guardrail) No RSVPs divided by invites sent Tantra campaign stats

Registrations lead because an accepted invite is not a sign-up: only a registration gives a WebinarJam join link.

Decision Rule

Read 48 hours after the session, once both variants have 40,000 sent. Run python3 ab_decide.py A_INVITES A_YESMAYBE A_NO A_REGISTERED B_INVITES B_YESMAYBE B_NO B_REGISTERED.

  1. Registration rates differ at p < 0.05 (two-proportion z-test): the higher one wins.
  2. Otherwise, Yes/Maybe rates differ at p < 0.05: the higher one wins.
  3. A winner whose No rate is more than 1 point above the loser's does not win.
  4. No winner: keep A (no merge tags, so nothing can render wrong) and test a new challenger against it next week.

The winner becomes the control. Each new challenger changes one thing (the title, or the opening line), never both, so the next result names its cause.

Tantra renders a merge tag with no value as blank (https://usetantra.com/help/templates-and-ai/merge-tags-and-spintax), and it has no if/else. So the fallbacks live in the contact import: a blank first name would read "Hi ,", a blank company "the pipeline at still runs". Only a company the list confirmed (company_source LinkedIn, or found on the homepage) goes in company; a guessed one ("Thekbbcompany") is replaced with your company.

Each link carries utm_source=calendar&utm_medium=invite&utm_campaign=enterprise-webinar&utm_content=cal_a (or cal_b), so Tantra's own Enable UTM Parameters stays off (it would add a second set).

Rules the Copy Follows

  • Titles are named like meetings: no "$", no "%", no "free", topic in the first 24 characters (the calendar grid cut). The webinar title, with its numbers, goes in the description.
  • Description: date, time and both zones first; who it is for and what they leave with; an easy decline line; then the link.
  • Patterns reused: context/from-workshop-project/12-calendar-invites/, 03-calendar-infra/calendar-invites.md and the anonymised real invites in context/from-tantra-content/sources/library/real-outreach.ts.
