Call 2 Script: Tantra Enterprise Proposal Review (30 Minutes)

Open: Call 2 length is open: 30 minutes now (the Cal.com event type), the sales scripts doc proposes 45; Adi decides. This script is timed for 30.

For Conor and Scott. The prospect has read your written proposal and booked this call from the proposal email, on your own Proposal Review link. You ran their Call 1, so you run this one. This is the call where the decision is made: by minute 30 you have a yes, a no, or the one thing still in the way, written down.

Approved by Adi on 2026-11-05 (the task board). Proof comes from PROOF_PACK.md only. Objection answers come from the FAQ: objections-faq.html (PDF: deliverables/25-sales-collateral/objections-faq-a4.pdf).

If the person who signs off is not there: "Is {name} joining? This is the call where we decide, so it's much easier with them here." If they cannot join, run the call and use the "someone else signs" row in Decide.

18-discovery-call-script.md

Discovery Call Script: Tantra Enterprise Evaluation Call (30 Minutes)

For Conor and Scott. The call follows the five promises in the booking description, in order, so the lead gets exactly what they were told they would get. Proof on this call comes from PROOF_PACK.md only.

This is Call 1. If it fits, the written proposal follows within one business day with your own Proposal Review link inside, and Call 2 (18-call-2-proposal-review-script.md) is where the decision is made. You run both.

Minutes Part Description line it delivers
0 to 2 Open "If it isn't, we'll tell you honestly on the call"
2 to 10 Map today "Map your offer, your buyers and your numbers today"
10 to 17 Find the leak "Find the stage where your funnel is losing the most revenue"
17 to 21 Pick the mechanism "Choose the conversion mechanism that fits your offer"
21 to 26 Scope "Agree on scope, timeline and who on your team will be involved"
26 to 30 Decide "Decide together, on the call, whether Tantra Enterprise is the right fit"

0 to 2: Open

Thanks for making the time, {first name}. Here's how I'd like to use the 30 minutes. First I'll get a clear picture of your offer and your numbers. Then we'll find the stage where your funnel loses the most revenue. Then, if it makes sense, I'll show you how we'd build it with you. And before we hang up we'll decide together whether Tantra Enterprise fits. If it doesn't, I'll tell you straight. Sound good?

Then use your prep: "Before we start, you said your biggest bottleneck is {bottleneck answer}. Is that still the one?"

2 to 10: Map Today

Ask, listen, write the numbers in Pipeline notes.

  1. What do you sell, at what price, and who buys it?
  2. Roughly what comes in a month now? (Check against their revenue band.)
  3. Where do new customers come from today? Of those, which channel would you bet on if you could only keep one?
  4. How does a stranger become a customer, step by step? (Traffic, then what?)
  5. Your numbers this month, as best you know them: how many people see you, how many raise a hand, how many get on a call, how many buy?
  6. Who on your team runs marketing and sales today?

10 to 17: Find the Leak

Walk the stages in order and stop where the drop is worst:

Stage Question
Traffic Is the problem that not enough of the right people see you?
Capture Of the people who see you, how many give you a name and email?
Show-up Of those, how many turn up to the call, event or demo?
Conversion Of the ones who turn up, how many buy?
Follow-up What happens to everyone who didn't buy the first time?

Then quantify it: "If that one stage worked twice as well, what would that be worth to you a month?" Let them do the math. Write their number down; you will use it in the decide step.

17 to 21: Pick the Mechanism

Match the offer to one of the three mechanisms in the description:

  • Live event (webinar): a considered purchase that needs teaching and trust; one presenter can sell to a room.
  • Video sales funnel: a simpler offer, or a team that can't host live every week.
  • Multi-day workshop: a high-ticket offer where buyers need to try the method before they commit.

Based on what you sold me on, I'd build {mechanism} first, because {their reason, in their words}.

Proof, only if it helps, one line, measured numbers only: - Growth Idea, a UK business-coaching group: 11 webinars, May 2025 to Feb 2026, 6,517 registered, 1,384 came live, 80 sales calls booked, 4 clients won. - FintechCircle, a London fintech network: outreach grew from 10,000 reached to 20,046 and then 30,000 calendar invites across three episodes (March to April 2026), which booked 17 calls.

Never quote a pipeline figure, a teaching-model number or a testimonial headline on the call.

21 to 26: Scope (Only If It Fits)

Describe what Tantra Enterprise is, then what it would mean for them.

Tantra Enterprise is $9,999. For that you get three months of 1:1 coaching and implementation with your team, a done-with-you build of your funnel, a year of access to the Tantra platform, and the full course, the community and the group coaching calls.

Then agree the three things the proposal needs: 1. Scope: which stages we build first (start from the leak you found). 2. Timeline: when they want the first event or funnel live. 3. People: who on their team owns it day to day, and who signs off.

26 to 30: Decide

Fit statement, then one question:

Here's what I heard. You're at {where they are}, the biggest leak is {stage}, and fixing it is worth about {their number} a month to you. We'd build {mechanism} with your team over three months. Does that sound like the right next step?

Answer Say Log in the CRM
Yes "Then I'll send your written proposal within one business day, with a link to book a short call with me to walk you through it. That's where we go through it together and decide. Who else should be on that call?" Notes, then Showed; then Proposal sent when it goes out (Seq. D starts and carries your Proposal Review link)
Yes, but someone else signs "Then let's make sure {them} is on the Proposal Review. I'll send the proposal within one business day with the link to book it." Notes, Showed; name the sign-off person in notes so the proposal goes to both
Not now "Understood. What would need to change for this to be the right time?" Notes with their answer, then Not ready (Seq. F starts)
Not a fit "Then I won't pitch you. Here's what I'd do in your position instead: {one honest suggestion}." Notes, Showed, then Closed Lost with reason Not a fit

Objections

Answer only the objection they raised, then ask one question back.

They say Answer Ask
"It's a lot of money." "It is. Against the number you gave me for fixing {stage}, how long would it take to pay back?" "If it paid back inside the three months, would it still be too much?"
"Not the right time." "Fair. The leak in {stage} costs about {their number} every month it stays open." "What happens between now and the right time?"
"We could build it ourselves." "You could. Enterprise is for teams who want it built with them, by people who have built it before, and running inside the business in three months." "Who on your team would build it, and when?"
"I need to think about it." "Of course. What specifically do you want to think through?" Answer that one thing. If it needs the proposal, promise it within one business day, and the Proposal Review to go through it together.

After the Call

Within one hour: notes, budget and objections in the Pipeline row, then the menu action that matches the outcome. A deal left in Call Booked or Confirmed stops every follow-up from working.

Rules for Every Sequence

  • Whatsapp hours: WhatsApp only between 08:00 and 20:00 in the lead's timezone (Cal.com attendee timezone, else the default_timezone setting). A step due outside the window waits for the next 08:00; a pre-call reminder that would land after the call starts is skipped.

  • Opt out: STOP in any channel tags the contact Do Not Contact and cancels every pending step.
  • Optional lines: A line that starts with ? is dropped when one of its merge fields is empty; otherwise the ? is removed.

18-speed-to-lead.md

Speed to Lead: Every Booking Confirmed Inside 5 Minutes

A booked call is a promise the lead made while they were excited. Every hour you wait, that excitement cools and the no-show risk climbs. This is the process Conor and Scott run so every Enterprise Evaluation Call is confirmed by a human inside 5 minutes.

Offer on the call: Tantra Enterprise, $9,999. Call 1: 30 minutes on Google Meet, booked through the Cal.com round-robin link (item 19). Promise: a written proposal within one business day, carrying the closer's own Proposal Review link. Call 2: the 30-minute Proposal Review with the same closer, where the decision is made (section 7; approved by Adi 2026-11-05). WhatsApp replaces SMS everywhere.

4. Pre-Call Research Checklist (10 Minutes, Due 2 Hours Before)

  • [ ] Webinar tab: did they attend, how many minutes, did they stay past minute 25, what did they answer in the polls, did they click the offer?
  • [ ] Their website: what they sell, at what price, to whom, and how a buyer gets in today (book a call, checkout, demo, application).
  • [ ] Their funnel: is there a webinar, a video sales letter, a lead magnet or a booking page? Note the step that looks weakest.
  • [ ] Their traffic: check the main channel they named against what you can see (founder's LinkedIn activity, ads, content).
  • [ ] Rough math: monthly revenue band divided by price point gives the customers a month they win now.
  • [ ] Sign-off: who signs, and is that person coming? If not, plan to ask for a second call with them.
  • [ ] Pick the one client example you may need, from PROOF_PACK only (Growth Idea or FintechCircle measured numbers).

  1. No-show rebooking (conflict). The playbook's Sequence C rebooks no-shows. Your corrected call description says: "If the call is missed without notice, we won't be able to reschedule it or arrange another conversation in the future."   2. Who is the setter? Default: the assigned closer confirms their own bookings. Name a separate setter in setter_name and setter_email if you want one.
  3. Proposal timing. The playbook says send it within 1 hour of the call; your description promises one business day. Built to the description, with the task telling closers to aim for the same day.
  4. How messages send.
