ICP_AND_SEGMENTS.md

Enterprise ICP and Segments for the Cold Calendar List

Who We Invite

The buyer of Tantra Enterprise ($9,999) wants the funnel built with them, with coaching, and has $10,000 or more to invest in the next 12 months (offer.fit in deliverables/_shared/funnel-config.json).

In practice that is the founder, owner or revenue lead of a B2B company where a person on a call closes the deal (context/from-tantra-content/02-target-audience.md, sections 1, 2 and 6):

  • B2B software and SaaS companies.
  • Consultancies, advisory firms and agencies (marketing, web, software).
  • Financial services: fintech, banking, wealth, insurance, accounting, venture capital.
  • Staffing and executive search.
  • Course and coaching businesses with a team behind them.

Not the buyer: consumer businesses, schools and universities, government, and anyone without $10,000 to put behind a build (they go to Pro or the Course).

What the Master CSV Can and Cannot Tell Us

Be clear about this before reading any tier: the master CSV has almost no job title or industry data. Of 2,752,479 rows, 6 have a job title, 3 a location and 6 a LinkedIn URL. We cannot filter by title, company size or country of the person.

What we can use:

Domain endings say nothing reliable about the person's country: a .com can be anywhere. Company names in the CSV are guessed from domains and are rough; calendar invites carry no merge fields, so they are never printed.

How the Segments Were Chosen

Two sources, read together:

  1. The reference segment sheet (the competitor's bookings by segment, path in README). It ranks consulting and advisory firms, marketing and SEO agencies, venture capital, financial services and wealth management highest on bookings per invite, and puts B2B software in the middle. It ranks healthcare, non-profits, legal services and an untargeted "general" bucket lowest, each soaking up large volume for few bookings. We take the ranking only, not its numbers.
  2. Our own past response by bucket (master CSV, count only). The buckets that answered Yes or Maybe most often include Banking, FinTech, Wealth, Web and Software, B2B, Insurance, Management and Consulting. Field Services, Logistics, IT Services, Engineering, Retail, Education and Government answered least. Tech and agency domain endings (.io, .ai, .co, .tech, .agency, .vc, .studio) answered Yes or Maybe at 1.3 to 2 times the rate of .com.

Both point the same way, and both match the buyer above.

Per-Domain Caps

At most 3 invites to one recipient domain in a week and 30 over the whole plan (ramp plus main). Large companies in the list hold up to 15,092 addresses on one domain; sending hundreds of cold invites into one company gets our senders blocked by that company's security filter. Every recipient domain is also tied to one sender pool, so one company only ever hears from one Workspace.

What This Gives

  • Cold usable: 2,037,463 rows. The 1,000,000-row main list is fully covered from the master: no gap.
  • The main list is 439,939 tier A rows (all of tier A that fits under the domain caps; 4,249 more go into the ramp weeks), then 560,061 tier B rows, almost all with high-confidence names. Tier C is never in the main list.
  • Warm list: 18,927 rows. Ramp weeks: 343,200 invites. Reserve after S10: 713,190 rows (591,418 inside the domain cap).
  • Re-invite gap: every row was last invited by other senders between 2026-07-22 and 2026-10-19; no row is scheduled less than 60 days after its last invite (SLICE_STATS.md).
  • Held for the EU and UK review: 393,755 rows (country endings only, so the hold is partial). If Adi clears some countries, rerun the script with those country endings removed from GDPR_HOLD.

The Honest Limits

  • Every row in the master was already invited once, in series E150 to E162, by senders that are not ours. Expect lower response than a fresh list.
  • Tier B is "B2B, segment unknown". Without titles we cannot tell a founder from an analyst.
  • For true Enterprise targeting by title and company size, buy a title-filtered list: VENDOR_PLAN.md.

SLICING_PLAN.md

The 10-Week, 100,000-a-Week Slicing Plan

Full volume cannot start on day one. Every sender Workspace sends no cold invites for three weeks, then 10 a sender a day, 25 from day 63 and 50 from day 91 (item 3, deliverables/03-outreach-infra/CAPACITY_PLAN.md). So the plan has two parts: 10 ramp weeks, then 10 main weeks at 100,000.

Assumptions Behind the Dates

  • Day 0 = Monday 9 November 2026: the day all four Workspaces have their $100 paid. Every date below moves by the same number of days if Day 0 moves (rerun the script with --day0).
  • 440 senders in 4 pools of 110 (P1 to P4, one Google Workspace each: W1 is the strategywebinars Workspace, W2 to W4 are new). Capacity at full volume is 110,000 a week; each main slice is 100,000.
  • 5 send days a week, Monday to Friday. No sends on 25 December 2026 or 1 January 2027.
  • Each week's invites are for the webinar of the following week ({{webinar.day_of_week}}, {{webinar.start_time_local}}; the event date is set in each Tantra campaign).

The Re-Invite Gap

Every row in the master was already invited by other senders between 2026-07-22 and 2026-10-19. The script puts no row into a week that starts less than 60 days after its last_invited_date, and ranks the oldest invites first. Rows still too recent when a week starts: R01 (30 Nov) 427,587; R02 254,513; R03 98,510; none from R04 (21 Dec) on. The main slices are not affected. Counts in SLICE_STATS.md, Re-invite gap.

Which Pool Sends Whom

Every recipient domain is tied to one pool by a fixed hash, so one company only hears from one Workspace. If a Workspace is suspended, only its pool's files wait; the other three keep sending.

Weeks 1 to 3: Internal Activity Only (team step)

No cold invites before day 21. In each of the first three weeks, every sender account does a little normal work so Google sees a real user:

  • Weeks 1 and 2: each sender accepts two or three internal calendar events a day sent by a teammate in the same Workspace, and joins one short Google Meet.
  • Week 3: each sender invites three to five known external contacts a day (partners, clients, team members' own work addresses) who accept.

Who does it: Adi names one team member per Workspace. Nothing is sent to the list.

Do We Need to Buy Rows for the 1M?

No. The master CSV gives 2,037,463 usable cold rows, so the 1,000,000-row main list and the 343,200 ramp invites come from it with no gap (SLICE_STATS.md).

We should still buy one list, for three reasons:

  1. No titles. The master cannot tell a founder from an analyst. A vendor list filtered by title and company size is the only way to aim at the $10,000+ buyer.
